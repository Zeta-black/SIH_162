# Judge Q&A

Fifteen questions a sharp evaluator is likely to ask, with answers that are true.

Rule for everyone answering: **if you do not know, say so and say what would be
needed to find out.** A confident wrong answer loses more than an honest "we
haven't validated that yet". Every number below is in `CLAIMS_REGISTER.md` with
its evidence.

---

### Q1. How is this different from the NASA FIRMS viewer, which is free?

FIRMS tells you *where* heat is. It does not tell you whether that heat is a
refinery doing its job or a refinery on fire — that is the actual question, and
it is PS 162's question.

Concretely, from today's demo: FIRMS shows both ArcelorMittal detections as hot
pixels, and the 36 MW one looks far more alarming than the 4 MW one. We invert
that, because we know the 36 MW signature repeats at that site every few days
and the 4 MW one has never appeared there before. FIRMS has no concept of
"normal for this site".

We also group pixels into sites: 716 detections become 390 sites, so you triage
incidents rather than pixels.

---

### Q2. Is the score a probability?

**No.** It is an uncalibrated Random Forest vote share for the leading trained
class. We label it "model score" everywhere, and the API says so in the payload
(`model.score_note`).

Turning it into a probability would require calibration against verified
outcomes — reliability curves, a Brier score — and we do not have per-detection
ground truth to calibrate against. So we did not claim it.

---

### Q3. What is your accuracy?

This is the question we most want to answer carefully, because the honest answer
is more useful than the flattering one.

Our model scores **1.0 accuracy, 1.0 macro-F1** on a held-out, region-grouped
split. **That number is circular and you should discount it.** The training
labels are generated by a nine-rule policy over the same features the model
sees, so a high score measures how faithfully the forest reproduces that policy
— not whether it classifies fires correctly. A depth-3 decision tree and a
logistic regression score comparably on the same split, which is the giveaway.

This is recorded in the artefact itself: `backend/ml/artifacts/metrics.json`
carries `"circularity_warning": true` and an `interpretation` field saying the
figures are not verified real-world accuracy.

**What we can honestly claim:** the pipeline reproduces a documented, inspectable
labelling policy, abstains when evidence is thin, and separates classification
from urgency. **What we cannot claim:** validated real-world classification
accuracy. That needs incident reports from plant operators or a state pollution
control board to label against, which is exactly the partnership this would need
to become real.

---

### Q4. So how did you validate anything?

Four ways, none of which is "we trusted the metric":

1. **Leakage and duplication checks.** 18 duplicate detections removed before
   the split; the split is grouped by region, so the same industrial cluster
   cannot appear in both train and test.
2. **Baseline comparison.** `backend/ml/validate.py` fits a depth-3 tree and a
   logistic regression on the same split. They score comparably — which is how
   we know the metric reflects the policy, not model skill.
3. **Behavioural tests.** 353 automated tests, including ones asserting that an
   abstained event is never shown as a confident alarm, that a replay is never
   labelled live, and that a rejected API key still puts data on the map.
4. **The evidence gate, measured.** On a live India-wide query in September —
   outside any crop-burning season — the ungated model called 810 of 900
   detections "agricultural burning" at a median score of 0.66. Of those, 800 had
   no land cover available and 804 had no facility within 12 km: it was voting
   on position and radiative power alone. With the gate applied that falls to
   164 and the rest are reported as unclassified with the reason stated.

---

### Q5. What is actually novel here? Facility baselines and Bayesian updating are standard.

They are, and we will not claim otherwise. We are not claiming a new algorithm.

What we think is worth your attention is a product decision: **the system is
built to be understood in ten seconds and to admit when it does not know.** A
first-time user reads a plain sentence, three reasons traceable to real fields,
and an explicit list of what is missing. 111 of 390 sites say "not sure". The
maths is one click away, not on the front page.

Similar ideas exist elsewhere. Our focus is making the output understandable and
honest in seconds, because an alert a safety officer cannot act on is not an
alert.

---

### Q6. Who is the user, and what do they do differently on Monday?

A plant safety officer or a state pollution control board regulator.

Today they either do not look at satellite thermal data, or they look at a raw
FIRMS feed with no industrial context. With this they open one screen, see which
of their sites is behaving unusually, read why in one sentence, and either
dispatch someone or mark it reviewed.

The honest limitation: we generate the alert **in-app only**. There is no SMS, no
email, no SCADA integration. A real deployment needs that, and it is a small
piece of work compared with the data problem.

---

### Q7. The satellite data could be hours old. Doesn't that make it useless?

For *suppression*, yes — the fire brigade will beat us there. We are not a
detection siren.

Two things it is good for. First, **anomaly surveillance**: a thermal source that
starts appearing at a plant that has never shown one is worth investigating even
a day later, because it may still be burning or may recur. Second,
**accountability and pattern-finding**: persistent flaring, unreported events,
sites whose behaviour changes over months.

The UI never hides the age. The card shows the satellite observation time and
the retrieval time as separate fields, because they are different facts. On a
VIIRS NRT product the typical lag is a few hours.

---

### Q8. What happens if NASA is down, or you have no internet?

We built for that, because a venue network is the most likely failure.

Three rungs, each honestly labelled:
1. **Live** — fetched from NASA in this request.
2. **Offline** — the last successful fetch, read from disk, shown with its real
   age. Survives a restart.
3. **Replay** — the committed archive of real historical detections.

The chip in the corner says which one you are looking at and is never allowed to
say "Live" unless NASA actually answered. Verified: with a deliberately wrong
API key the app serves 811 real cached detections labelled "Offline", instead of
the empty map under a green "Live" badge that it used to show.

You can test it right now — turn off the Wi-Fi and press reload.

---

### Q9. How does this scale beyond the facilities you have?

Honestly: the current archive holds **1,482 industrial facilities across six
regions** — Jamnagar, Hazira, Singrauli, Korba, central Punjab and Similipal. We
do not claim national coverage and the UI frames the map to the data we have
rather than to an India-wide box, deliberately.

Scaling is a data problem, not an architecture problem. Facilities come from
OpenStreetMap via Overpass; extending to all of India means more Overpass
queries and a bigger cache, which is an afternoon. The real work is that OSM
industrial tagging is uneven, so a national rollout would want an authoritative
facility register — a CPCB or state industries-department list — rather than
crowd-sourced tags.

Compute is not the constraint: clustering 716 detections takes about 100 ms on a
laptop.

---

### Q10. What are the limitations? Be specific.

In rough order of how much they would bother us:

1. **No verified ground truth.** Labels are rule-generated. See Q3.
2. **`INDUSTRIAL_FIRE` is rare in training** — 72 examples out of 3,514, with 8
   in the test set. Any claim about that specific class is weakly supported.
3. **Abnormality is not yet a true per-facility baseline.** It currently reads
   "no prior detection" as "unusual", which conflates a quiet site with a site
   we have no coverage of. Documented in `docs/BASELINE.md` D1; the verdict layer
   deliberately makes no "N times normal" claims until it is fixed.
4. **Land cover is missing for roughly half the detections** — OSM has no
   land-use polygon there. We report "not mapped", never a guess.
5. **No optical confirmation.** Sentinel-2 is not wired up. The evidence drawer
   says "Optical confirmation: not available" on every single event rather than
   leaving you to assume it was checked.
6. **Alerts are in-app only.** No SMS, email or SCADA.
7. **Facility association is proximity, not causation**, and the UI says so.

---

### Q11. Why should I believe the "not sure" cases are real abstention and not a bug?

The threshold is a published setting (`UNKNOWN_ABSTENTION_THRESHOLD=0.45`,
visible in `/api/meta`), chosen on validation data, not tuned to make the demo
look good. `UNKNOWN` is not a trained class — it is applied at inference when the
top class does not clear the bar.

You can check the arithmetic on screen: the coal-mine example scores 0.4431, the
threshold is 0.45. Open "Details for experts" and the number is there.

There is also a second, stricter gate: when the discriminating evidence is
*absent* — no facility in radius and no land cover — the bar rises, because the
forest is then voting mostly on position and power. Q4 has the measured effect.

---

### Q12. Could a plant operator game this?

Partly, and it is worth being clear about how.

Since persistence lowers priority, a site that flares constantly sinks down the
queue — which is correct for incident triage but means routine flaring becomes
invisible to *this* screen. A regulator cares about exactly that, so a
compliance view would need to invert the ranking. We have not built it.

What cannot easily be gamed is the satellite observation itself: FIRMS is a
third-party feed the operator does not control.

---

### Q13. Why Random Forest and not deep learning?

We have 3,514 labelled rows with rule-generated labels. A neural network would
reproduce the same policy with less interpretability and more ways to go wrong,
and it would still be bounded by the label quality, not the model class.

The forest also gives per-feature contributions that we surface as the "why"
lines. That matters more than a point of F1 here — and given Q3, a point of F1
on this metric would mean nothing anyway.

---

### Q14. What did you change since round 1?

The evaluator feedback was: show something live, and the UI has far too much
content.

- The landing screen used to be a wall of six KPI tiles, a 30-row table and two
  charts, with **no map on it at all**. It is now one map with four floating
  panels and three numbers.
- Added the plain-language verdict, the three traceable reasons, and the
  explicit "not sure about" line.
- Made the demo survive a dead network: the on-disk cache, the honest fallback
  chip, and `npm run demo` as a single command.
- Fixed real honesty bugs found on the way — four sites claimed an "episodic,
  not routine" signature directly above evidence saying "detected on 3 separate
  days"; absent land cover was being listed as *supporting* evidence.

Before-and-after screenshots are in `docs/screens/`.

---

### Q15. Can I try it myself?

Yes, and please do.

```
npm run demo:offline
```

One command, no API key needed, no network needed. It installs what is missing,
starts both halves, pre-warms the archive and opens a browser. Roughly 20
seconds if dependencies are already installed.

`npm run smoke` runs the full quality gate — 50 checks across secrets, build,
353 unit tests, every API route, the degradation matrix, and a real browser pass
with axe accessibility.

## The two questions we would ask if we were you

**"Your best number is 1.0 and you are telling me to ignore it. So what is left?"**
A working pipeline on real data, an inspectable policy, a system that abstains
111 times out of 390, and a UI a stranger can read in ten seconds. We would
rather show you that than a number we cannot defend.

**"What would make this real?"**
Verified incident labels from an operator or a pollution control board. One
season of those turns every "we cannot claim" in this document into something
measurable. Everything else here is already built.
